## Principles
Story before concept. Business before implementation. Mental models before
terminology. Vendor neutrality always.
Every chapter answers: why does this exist, who pays for it, what happens when
it breaks.

## Voice
Match voice/sample.md. These rules override any default style:
- No em dashes.
- Active voice with human subjects. People do things. Systems do not happen.
- No adverbs.
- No "not X, it's Y" constructions.
- No throat-clearing openers. Start on the concrete noun.
- Specific nouns over vague declaratives. "The Mumbai region" beats "the cloud
  environment".
- Vary sentence length. Short sentences carry weight.
- Narrator: a tour guide who has run the infrastructure. Observationally funny.
  No motivational language, no corporate jargon, no gatekeeping, no vendor
  evangelism, no exam-prep phrasing, no implementation tutorials.

## Hard content rules
- Never state vendor prices. Never quote SKU-level tiers or quotas.
- Any figure that can go stale carries the year it was true.
- Every chapter contains at least one first-hand story. When it is not written
  yet, insert `<!-- STORY-TODO: what this needs -->` and move on. Never invent
  an anecdote. Never attribute one to the author.
- Borrowed case studies (Netflix, Spotify, Airbnb, Shopify, Capital One) fill at
  most half of any chapter's examples. The reader can get those free in thirty
  seconds. The other half comes from the author's own work.
- Analogies: airports, warehouses, hotels, utilities, transportation, supply
  chains. No fantasy, no sci-fi, no cartoons.
- Grayscale-safe figures only. No color-dependent meaning.
