## The page-count question (self-publishing)

At a compressed length, a book can look thin beside padded incumbents, and
browsers equate thickness with value. **Do not solve it with padding.** Solve it
in the copy and the cover: lead with time-to-value, and let reference appendices
justify a premium price. Confirm the extent clears the print service's spine-text
threshold (KDP: 79 pages) so it does not look amateur shelved spine-out.

## Keeping it from going stale

TODO: If the subject moves, name your defences. In order of strength:

- Do not enumerate the perishable things (prices, versions, screenshots). Point
  the reader to current primary sources instead of printing a number that dates.
- Original, evergreen examples and case studies rather than borrowed ones that
  get retired or rotated.
- Quarantine the fastest-moving material into clearly-marked chapters written at
  the decision level, so a revision is cheap and localised.

Self-publishing turns staleness from a liability into an advantage: a revised
edition ships the week the facts change. Keep the repo ready to rebuild.
