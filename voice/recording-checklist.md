# Recording checklist

The BRIEF asks for three voice notes. This maps the STORY-TODO markers now sitting
in live drafts onto those three prompts, so one 25-to-30-minute session covers the
most ground. Do not prepare. Do not restart. Talk the way you would at lunch.

The rule that matters: **tell the true one.** The prompts below give options
because a marker will accept any of several stories. Pick the one that actually
happened to you. Never stretch to hit all of them.

---

## Note 1 — The night something broke (covers 2 markers)

Two failure stories live in the drafts. Tell whichever one you actually lived; if
you have both, tell both.

- **The room that ran out of cooling.** (Chapter 1 opener.) A launch or a sale
  filled the room past what it could cool. The phone rang. The first twenty
  minutes you blamed the wrong thing. From the ring to the fix, including being
  wrong.
- **The half-succeeded write.** (Article 02.) The file landed but the record
  never got written, so there was an object nobody could find and nobody could
  delete, quietly costing money. From the first confused ticket to the moment
  someone realised the two halves had come apart.

Include: a time, a place, a role of a person, and the number that made it real.

## Note 2 — The belief that is wrong, and what it costs (covers 3 markers)

Three misconceptions are set up in the drafts. Each wants a short story of the
belief meeting reality.

- **"The cloud is nowhere."** (Article 03.) A time the country the data lived in
  turned out to matter: a customer who needed records to stay in-jurisdiction, an
  auditor who asked where a table physically sat, or a region choice that was
  really a compliance decision.
- **"The vendor raised prices."** (Article 11.) The bill line that grew and the
  product decision behind it. The feature that shipped to applause and tripled a
  cost line nobody watched, or the leftover thing running for months.
- **"Compute is infinite."** (Article 12.) Being inside the capacity scramble.
  The region that came back with a date instead of a yes. The moment a switch
  turned out to be a supply chain with a lead time.

## Note 3 — What you actually do all day (covers the register, plus 1 marker)

Explain your job to a cousin who sells insurance and has never heard of a server.
This one is less about a single marker and more about capturing the plain voice
that the whole book borrows. It also naturally surfaces:

- **The residency question answered live.** (Article 03, second angle.) The
  moment on a real call where "where does it live" stopped being abstract and
  became a line item you had to own.

## After recording

1. Transcribe with Whisper. Delete filler and nothing else. No tidying.
2. Paste the transcript into `voice/sample.md`, replacing the stub.
3. Tell me it is in. I will lift the real beats into the five markers, replacing
   the placeholder scenes, and we compare the before and after.
