# Buyer

The one reader this book is for. Write this before the TOC; it wins any argument
about scope. A book with a precise reader can stay short, because it can skip
what that reader already knows. A book for "everyone" serves no one and has no
purchase trigger.

Replace every bracket. Keep the structure — each section does a job.

## The one person

**[Name. Role. Context specific enough to picture — company size, tenure, how

makes them feel exposed, stuck, or wrong. Specific scenes, not categories. The
scenes are what every chapter's opening will pay off.]

## What they can do after finishing the book

[The outcomes, numbered O1, O2, ... Each is a thing the reader can *do* at work
or in life afterwards, not a thing they will *know*. The kill test scores every
chapter against this list; a chapter that maps to none of these is in trouble.]

- **O1** [capability]
- **O2** [capability]
- **O3** [capability]

## Why they do not just ask a chatbot

[The single most important section. If a model can answer the reader's question
from a one-line prompt, the book has no reason to exist. Name what the reader
needs that generated text cannot supply: sequence, judgment about what matters,
first-hand stories of what goes wrong. This is the book's moat — protect it in
every chapter.]

## What they already know, so the book must not re-explain it

[The prerequisite list. Being explicit here is what lets the book skip the
foundations and stay dense. A chapter that re-teaches something on this list has
lost the reader.]

## Where they are not

[The anti-reader, stated as boundaries. What this reader does NOT want — e.g. a
certification, a tutorial, a reference manual. Name where they will silently quit
if the book drifts.]

## Secondary buyers, same book

[Other people who buy the same book for their own reason. They do not change the
book, but they widen the market. Name them and their one reason to buy.]

## The bulk buyer (if any)

[The institutional purchaser who buys many copies at once — a manager for a team,
a course for students. This buyer can be worth more than months of individual
sales, and often needs one extra asset (a discussion guide, exercises). If they
exist, name what they need, and make sure nothing in the book costs them
anything.]

## The purchase moment

[When and why they buy. The trigger. This tells the cover and the marketing copy
what feeling to name. "Names the feeling, not the topic" is usually the stronger
cover.]

## The open bet

[What you are assuming about this reader that could be wrong, and how you will
find out — e.g. publish pilot articles and watch who forwards them. Name the
evidence that would confirm the direction and the evidence that would reopen it.]
