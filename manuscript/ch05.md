## Standard against Autopilot

- Who owns nodes, and what that does to the bill
- Autopilot charges for pod requests rather than nodes, making this a cost
  decision as much as an operational one
- Where Autopilot's constraints bite: privileged pods, DaemonSets, node
  customisation

## Release channels and upgrades

- Rapid, regular, stable, and picking one as an architecture decision
- Maintenance windows and exclusions
- Surge upgrade settings against disruption budgets

## Cluster topology

- Zonal, regional, and control plane availability
- Private clusters, control plane CIDR, and authorised networks
- Node pools, taints, and GPU affinity for accelerated work

## Identity

- Workload Identity Federation for GKE: pods assume a Google service account
  with no key material. Cross-reference chapter 2
- The wrong answers all distribute credentials

- Fleet as the unit of multi-cluster management

- Cloud Service Mesh, and when the complexity pays

## Exam traps

- A team without Kubernetes experience: capability is a legitimate
  architectural input, and Cloud Run is often the correct answer
- "No credentials in the image" means Workload Identity
- Strong pod-level isolation requirements point at GKE Sandbox
